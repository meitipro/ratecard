---
title: Writing good anchors
description: The five example jobs are the product. How to write a ladder that sizes the same way every time.
---

The ladder is five short descriptions of real past work, one per tier, smallest to largest. Every claim is compared with them, so a card is exactly as fair as its anchors.

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Anchors | exactly five, XS to XL |
| One anchor | one line, at most 200 characters |
| Prices | one per tier, in wei, and they do not fall as the tier rises |

## What makes an anchor good

- **Real work, plainly named.** "Run a weekly community call with notes" is better than "community work". A claim is compared by scope and effort, so the anchor should name both.
- **Evenly spaced.** If S and M describe nearly the same job, a claim between them will flip between tiers. The card form shows which two anchors sit closest together.
- **Different in kind of effort, not in polish.** The judge is told that size is scope and effort, not quality or how well it is written.

## What the judge does with them

The prompt carries the five anchors in order and one claim fenced as data, and asks for the tier whose example the claim is closest to. **Between two tiers it picks the smaller.** That is a deliberate tie break, so a pool is never overspent by a generous reading. If a claim is larger than the XL example, the answer is XL.

## Fixed once the card opens

Anchors and prices cannot change after `open_card`. A new ladder is a new card. This is on purpose: a rigged ladder is visible to every contributor before any claim, and nobody can reprice a claim after the fact.

## Anchors are public

They are shown on the card page and read by the validators as data: every `<` and `>` in an anchor is replaced at the prompt boundary, so an anchor cannot close its own block and open a forged one.
